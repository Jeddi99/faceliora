3D face scanning with an iPhone, using Faceliora Scan

A 3D face scan for Faceliora Clinic is made with the Faceliora Scan app on an iPhone, because Faceliora Clinic does not scan faces itself. The clinic scans the client with Faceliora Scan and opens the scan in a case; a clinic that already uses another scanner can import GLB, OBJ or ZIP files instead. A good scan is the start of a good simulation, so this page explains how to make one.

Key points

  • Scan with Faceliora Scan on an iPhone, then send the scan to Faceliora Clinic.
  • A still face, tied-back hair and even light matter more than the phone.
  • Consent is recorded before a phone scan is kept in the clinic’s record.

From iPhone to case

In Faceliora Scan, the person scanning signs in with the clinic’s Faceliora Clinic account, chooses before (a new case) or after (an existing case), chooses the patient and confirms consent if none is on record. After capture the app builds the 3D model and sends it to the clinic, and Faceliora Clinic lists it in its phone-scan inbox for the clinic to open. Sending through the account depends on the version of Faceliora Scan installed; where it cannot yet, save the scan as a file and import it into Faceliora Clinic.

Before the scan

Preparing the same way every time makes scans easier to compare.

  • Use an iPhone that Faceliora Scan supports; a self-scan needs a TrueDepth front camera.
  • Tie the hair back, clear of the forehead, the ears and the jaw line.
  • Keep the face still with the same expression, and no blinking until the scan ends.
  • Use even room light: close the curtains and switch off any lamp beside the client.
  • Clip the grey card or ColorChecker the clinic was given flat on the chest, facing the camera.

Capturing the face

Hold the iPhone in landscape and move around the still face as the markers on screen ask, front, left, right, high and low, until there are enough clear photos, usually 30–60, and check the coverage before finishing. For a self-scan, keep the phone still and turn the head as the spoken directions ask.

Importing files from another scanner

A scan from another app can be opened as a GLB file, an OBJ file, or a ZIP holding an OBJ with its skin texture. The skin’s colour comes from the texture, and millimetres come from the face width the clinic confirms on the scan, so the unit used in the file does not matter.

Consent and privacy

A 3D face identifies a person, so under Thailand’s PDPA it is personal data. The clinic’s system keeps a phone scan only for a patient whose consent is recorded, each clinic’s data is kept apart, and the system logs who opened or changed a case. The simulation is computed on the clinic’s computer. Explaining the purpose to the client and obtaining consent remain the clinic’s duties.

What a scan records

A scan records the shape and colour of the outer skin, not muscle, fat or bone. Light, movement and hair change what it records, which is why the same preparation each time matters. Our article on phone 3D face scans covers this with the research behind it.

Every view in Faceliora Clinic is a simulation for communication, not a guaranteed result, and does not replace a physician’s examination and advice.

3D face scanning with an iPhone: questions

Does Faceliora Clinic scan the face itself?

No. Faceliora Clinic receives scans from the Faceliora Scan app on an iPhone, or opens GLB, OBJ and ZIP files from another scanner.

Which iPhone do I need?

One that Faceliora Scan supports. A self-scan needs a TrueDepth front camera, and building the 3D model on the phone depends on the model, so check your iPhone and your version of the app with our team before you start.

How does a scan reach the clinic?

In a version that links to the clinic account, Faceliora Scan sends the scan into the clinic’s record under the chosen patient. Every version can save the scan as a file to import into Faceliora Clinic. Which routes are available depends on the version of the app, so check with our team before you start.

What makes a scan good enough for simulation?

A still face with the same expression, hair tied back, even light with no window or side lamp, and every side of the face covered. A grey card in view helps with colour.

Is consent required?

Yes. The clinic’s system keeps a phone scan only for a patient whose consent is recorded, and Faceliora Scan asks for consent before sending if none is on record.
