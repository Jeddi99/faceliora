3D skin analysis from a face scan

Book a demoFaceliora Clinic overview

3D skin analysis in Faceliora Clinic reads the client’s 3D face scan and highlights where each skin concern was found, the way a skin analyser presents it, so a clinician can walk the client through it one concern at a time. It uses the scan the clinic already has, with no extra photograph. Every finding is for a clinician’s review, not a diagnosis.

Capability details are as described by the previous website; not yet verified by product testing.

Key points

  • Concerns found on the scan: pores, blackheads, spots, melasma-like patches, acne-like red spots, redness and wrinkles, with folds and tear troughs measured on the 3D shape.
  • Three simulated lights: daylight, red for blood under the skin, and simulated UV for pigment.
  • Amounts are “a few”, “some” or “many”, never a grade, and every result is for a clinician’s review.

What the analysis looks for

When the skin tab opens, the face shows every concern found at once; choosing a concern from the menu shows it alone, with its own report.

  • Enlarged pores and blackheads
  • Dark spots, moles and freckles
  • Melasma-like brown patches
  • Acne-like red spots
  • Redness (erythema)
  • Lines and wrinkles
  • Nasolabial folds and tear troughs, measured on the scan’s 3D shape
  • Dark circles and facial proportions

Do I need an extra photograph or device?

No. The analysis reads the 3D scan the clinic already has and needs no photograph, though it cannot show what only a dedicated skin-imaging camera captures.

Presented as a clinical report presents it

A mark you can point to, such as a dark spot, a red papule or a wide pore, is ringed where it was found; a blackhead is a dot, and uncertain marks are fainter. Pigment and redness are drawn as areas, wrinkles as lines, and folds and troughs as measured regions. The panel gives the clinical term, a table by the regions of the scale clinicians use, such as GAGS for acne and mMASI for melasma, the list of marks, and the method used to find them.

Amounts are given as “a few”, “some” or “many”. The page gives no skin grade or score, because a scan cannot provide everything a clinician’s scale asks for.

Is this a skin diagnosis?

No. The analysis proposes where concerns appear on the scan for a clinician to review. It gives no diagnosis, grade or score, and does not replace an examination.

Daylight, red light and simulated UV

Three light keys beside the face switch between daylight, red light and simulated UV. The red and UV views are simulations: the room’s light is taken out first, then the scan’s colour is separated into melanin and haemoglobin, so blood under the skin and pigment can be shown on the face. Redness opens on the red view and melasma on the UV view.

They are not UV photographs. Fluorescence, oil and the other readings of a booth analyser need its own camera and are not shown.

What do the red and UV views show?

They are simulations computed from the scan’s colour: the red view shows where blood under the skin makes an area redder than the rest of the face, and the simulated UV view shows pigment. Neither is a real UV photograph.

Computed on the clinic’s computer

The analysis runs on the clinic’s computer from the scan, with no photograph and nothing sent away to be computed. A summary is kept on the server only for a case whose client has given consent, and nothing skin-related is used for usage analytics.

Is the skin data sent anywhere?

The analysis is computed on the clinic’s computer. A summary is kept on the clinic’s server only for a case whose client has consented, and none of it is used for usage analytics.

What the analysis is and is not

The skin in a phone scan is smoother than in a high-resolution photograph from a booth analyser, so very small marks can be missed, and what is found is a proposal for the clinician to confirm. The analysis does not diagnose a condition or recommend a treatment.

Every view in Faceliora Clinic is a simulation for communication, not a guaranteed result, and does not replace a physician’s examination and advice.

Simulation is not diagnosis, prediction or a guarantee of treatment outcomes.
